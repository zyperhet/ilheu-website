---

Hun-Chung Lee's ceramics defy easy categorisation. Working at scales that challenge the very definition of pottery, his monumental vessels — some standing over a metre tall — occupy a space between ancient craft tradition and contemporary sculptural practice.

Born in Seoul and trained in both Korea and Europe, Lee's work has attracted an extraordinary following. His collectors include Brad Pitt, Norman Foster, and several major museum collections. Yet it is his relationship with the Azores that has defined his most recent and ambitious body of work.

Lee is currently planning ceramic interventions across all nine Azorean islands — site-specific works that respond to the volcanic geology and maritime culture of each island. "The Azores are made of fire and water," he explains. "Ceramics are born from the same elements. There is a deep kinship."
