Sofia Ribeiro splits her time between a research laboratory at the University of the Azores and the open ocean. A marine biologist specialising in the extremophile organisms that thrive around the archipelago's deep-sea hydrothermal vents, she has published in Nature, Science, and Deep-Sea Research.

But Ribeiro is equally at home writing for a general audience. Her essays on marine ecology have appeared in Granta, Orion Magazine, and The Guardian's Long Read. She believes that the Azores — sitting atop the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, where tectonic plates pull apart and new ocean floor is born — are one of the most important and least understood ecosystems on Earth.
