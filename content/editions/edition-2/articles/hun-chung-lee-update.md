Two years after we first spoke with Hun-Chung Lee about his plan to create ceramic installations across all nine islands of the Azores, the project has reached its halfway point. Installations on Pico and Flores were completed in late 2025, joining the São Miguel piece that served as the project's pilot.

The Pico vessel — a towering, dark-glazed form placed on a basalt outcrop overlooking the island's eponymous 2,351-metre volcano — draws its colour and texture from the island's black lava fields. The Flores piece, by contrast, is pale and fluid, inspired by the cascading waterfalls that define the island's western cliffs.

"Each island demands a different conversation," Lee says. "Pico is gravity and mass. Flores is movement and light. You cannot impose a single language on nine different personalities."

*First images of the Pico and Flores installations, plus Lee's plans for the remaining four islands, appear in Ilhéu Magazine Edition 2.*
